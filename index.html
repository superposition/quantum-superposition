Definitions and scope
Concepts Distinction Related models
Amplitude and probability An amplitude has magnitude and phase. For a normalized quantum state, the probability of a chosen measurement outcome is the square of the magnitude of its amplitude in that measurement basis. InterferenceComplex amplitude
Superposition and mixture A pure superposition has a definite relative phase. A statistical mixture can reduce or lose this phase coherence. Equal chances of zero and one do not by themselves specify a state. Pure statesDephasing
State space and physical space The Bloch sphere and its interior describe the states of one qubit. A position in this drawing determines measurement statistics; it does not locate a particle in physical space. Probability sections
Dephasing and measurement Dephasing can reduce coherence without recording an outcome. An ideal measurement with a recorded outcome leaves one copy in the corresponding measurement state. DephasingMeasurement
One copy and many copies A single measurement records one outcome. Batches estimate probabilities using fresh preparations. Repeating an ideal measurement of the same copy in the same basis gives the same result. Measurement
Classical logic and quantum gates A classical NOT gate exchanges zero and one. Pauli X does the same to these two quantum states, and also acts on their superpositions. Every gate in this viewer is reversible and preserves the length of a pure state direction. Clifford gates
A state and an operator A state determines the probabilities of measurement results. An operator describes a measurement or a transformation. The same Pauli operator can be used to describe either a measurement or a gate, but applying a gate does not record a measurement result. Pauli operators
Clifford gates and general gates Hadamard and phase generate all single-qubit Clifford actions. Starting at zero, these gates reach only the six marked stabilizer states. A gate outside this set, such as the T gate with its eighth turn around Z, can leave these six directions. Clifford gatesPauli operators
Overall phase and operator sign An overall phase change leaves the probabilities of an isolated qubit unchanged. Reversing a measurement operator exchanges its positive and negative outcome labels. The operator table keeps track of these signs. Pauli operators
One qubit and entanglement Clifford circuits can entangle multiple qubits using gates such as controlled NOT. A single Bloch sphere cannot describe those joint correlations. The models here contain one qubit. Clifford gates

State preparation

The balance slider sets the chance of one in a Z measurement. The phase slider sets the angle of the one amplitude relative to the zero amplitude. Together they specify a pure qubit state, apart from an overall phase that does not affect its measurement probabilities.

Clifford gates change this shared preparation. The operator view tracks the complete gate sequence, independently of the chosen starting state. Undo removes the last gate; restore returns to the starting state.

Model limits

The wave field is an illustration in arbitrary units. It does not solve the full time evolution of a physical quantum system. Sphere rotation changes only the viewing angle.

The term Clifford here refers to the quantum gate group. The site does not cover general Clifford algebras. The gates are ideal, and their internal physical operation is not simulated.
